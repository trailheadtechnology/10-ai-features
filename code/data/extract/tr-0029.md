Life list bird number 400 was supposed to be a great gray owl and instead it was a black-backed woodpecker, and I have decided to be at peace with this.

Context for non-birders who wandered in here looking for trail beta (it's below, I promise): Yosemite is one of the few places in California with a resident population of great gray owls, the largest owl in North America by length, a ghost in a tree costume, my white whale. I've dipped on this bird four times. So when I got a free weekend in early May I drove up specifically to work the meadow edges at dawn, and I picked the Peregrine Meadow Loop out past the valley because a guy on the county birding listserv reported "possible GGOW" there in April, and birders will drive three hundred miles for the word "possible."

May 9th. I was on trail at 5:15 a.m., headlamp off by 5:40, moving slow along the meadow edge in that gray pre-light when every stump is an owl. Every stump, folks. I raised my binoculars at eleven separate owl-shaped stumps. The meadow did its slow reveal — frost on the sedges, then color, then a coyote trotting the far edge with that busy commuter energy — and no owl. What I got instead, working a burned patch of lodgepole on the loop's north side, was a black-backed woodpecker excavating breakfast at close range, utterly indifferent to me, flaking off bark scales like a tiny demolition crew. Number 400. Not the bird I ordered. A great bird anyway. Birding teaches you this over and over: the day gives what it gives.

Full trip list highlights, because I know at least three of you skim for this: white-headed woodpecker (2), pileated (heard), mountain quail calling up-slope (never saw them, never do), Townsend's solitaire, MacGillivray's warbler skulking exactly like the field guide says, evening grosbeaks bombing around the trailhead parking lot like they owned it, and a golden eagle high over the granite around 10 a.m. Forty-one species by noon. The Merlin app and I disagreed once about an empid and I'm right.

Okay, actual trail information for normal humans. The Peregrine Meadow Loop runs something like 6 km — the park sign converts it to 3.7 miles — essentially flat, maybe 400 feet of rolling gain total, circling a wet meadow with lodgepole and red fir forest on the perimeter and one rocky viewpoint knob on the south end worth the tiny spur climb. In early May the trail was 90% clear with a few soggy stretches and two patches of rotten snow in the shaded north-side forest, ankle deep, posthole-adjacent by afternoon. The meadow itself is snow-free but saturated — stay on the trail, both for the habitat and for your socks. One creek crossing on stones that was straightforward in the morning and noticeably pushier at 1 p.m. with the melt coming up. Mosquitoes: present, not yet organized. Give them three weeks.

Non-bird wildlife: the aforementioned coyote, a ridiculous abundance of chickarees scolding me from every third tree, mule deer (6), and fresh bear sign on the north loop — a torn-up log and a print in the mud that stopped me mid-stride — though no bear. Made noise the rest of the loop like a polite one-man parade.

Crowds: I saw two trail runners and one other birder all morning, and the other birder and I did the thing where you sidle up and say "anything good?" like spies exchanging codes. He'd heard the great gray a week earlier. A WEEK. He showed me the spot. I stood there for forty minutes at dusk that evening and heard wind, robins, and my own heartbeat.

So: no owl at 400. The list rolls on. But I watched a meadow wake up from frost to full sun, and there are worse ways to fail at something. Great gray, I know you're in there. I'll be back in June, and I'm bringing a folding chair and unresolved feelings.
