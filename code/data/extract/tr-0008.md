Splits or it didn't happen. Alum Cave Trail to Mount LeConte, June 14, out-and-back, watch data below, excuses further below.

**The attempt:** LeConte via Alum Cave is my benchmark mountain run. 11 miles round trip, about 2,750 feet of climb to the summit area, and my PR from two years ago was 2:19 car to car. Goal for the day: sub-2:15. Spoiler: the mountain had notes.

**Conditions:** June in the Smokies means you start in a sauna and finish in a cloud. 66 degrees and 100 percent humidity at the Alum Cave trailhead at 6:05am. The trail was wet from overnight rain — the log bridges over Alum Cave Creek in the first mile were greasy, and I mean ice-rink greasy, so the first split was cautious. Above Arch Rock the rock steps were seeping, and the cable sections near the bluffs were carrying actual running water in two spots. From Gracie's Pulpit up, fog thick enough to chew.

**Splits, outbound:**
- Mile 1 (creek section, bridges): 10:42. Slow. Slick wood, no regrets.
- Mile 2 (through Arch Rock, up to Inspiration Point): 13:15. It kicks up hard here.
- Mile 2.3, Alum Cave Bluffs: passed two guys with headlamps who cheered, which I ran on for a solid mile, thanks guys.
- Mile 3 (bluffs to the cable traverses): 14:58. Walked the wet cables. I like my skull.
- Mile 4: 13:40.
- Mile 5 to the Lodge junction: 12:51.
- Tagged the summit area at 1:09:10. Eight seconds up on PR pace. Feeling like a genius, which on a mountain is always the prelude to the lesson.

**The lesson:** turned around at the top, opened up the stride, and 200 yards into the descent came around a bend directly into a bear. Yearling, maybe 80 pounds, dead center of the trail, eating something and completely uninterested in my training goals. And where there's a yearling there's a mama, so I stopped, backed up, made noise, and stood there doing jazz hands at a teenager bear for — I checked the watch, because of course I did — six minutes and forty seconds. It eventually shuffled off downslope. Somewhere behind the fog, mama presumably shrugged.

Six-forty. There went sub-2:15.

**Splits, inbound, post-bear:** descended angry, which is my best descending mood. 9:05, 9:22 (walked the cables again, still not sorry), 8:47, 10:01 through the Arch Rock steps because those are ankle-snappers when wet, 9:12 across the greasy bridges. Passed maybe forty hikers coming up by then — 8:30am on a June Saturday, the conga line was forming. Everybody was cool about yielding except one gentleman who did not grasp that a person moving at 9-minute pace downhill cannot stop instantly, sir.

**Final:** 2:22:41 car to car. Three and a half minutes off PR, six-forty of it spent in bear negotiations, so I'm calling it a moral PR and the record books can fight me.

**Other wildlife, non-obstructive division:** a peregrine screaming somewhere above the bluffs, juncos everywhere up top, one red squirrel that paced me for ten yards like it wanted to race, and about a million snails on the wet trail that I genuinely tried not to step on, which should count as agility training.

**Beta for runners:** the bridges and cable sections stay slick until midday after rain, budget for it. Water: I carried one soft flask and it was enough in the fog, would carry two on a clear day. Start before 6:30 or you will spend the descent apologizing your way through crowds. And build bear time into your goal pace, because this is the Smokies, highest bear density in the country, and the bears do not care about your watch.

Back in October for the leaf-season attempt. Sub-2:15 is happening. The yearling knows what it did.
