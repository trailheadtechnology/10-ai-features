# Mills Bench Lakes: a post-mortem

I'm a data engineer, I plan hikes in a spreadsheet, and on June 13th the spreadsheet and the mountain had a disagreement that I am writing up in the format I understand best. My hiking partner Grace has reviewed this document and her only comment was "you are unwell," which I'm interpreting as approval.

## Planned vs. actual

The plan, per the sheet: Mills Bench Lakes Trail, out-and-back, 8.6 miles, ~1,900 ft gain, start 0600, summit lake by 0915, car by 1230, burrito by 1315. Confidence: high. I'd cross-referenced three trip reports, snowpack telemetry from the nearest SNOTEL station, and webcam frames from the previous four days.

The actual: 7.1 miles, ~1,600 ft, turned around at the second lake, car by 1150. Burrito on schedule (some pipelines are sacred).

## Root cause analysis

The variance came down to snow. My model said the trail would be clear to about 10,400 feet based on the melt curve, and it WAS clear to almost exactly there — I want partial credit — but what the model didn't capture was the condition of the remaining snow above the second lake. Mid-June, north-facing aspects, and by 0930 the surface was rotten enough that we were postholing to mid-thigh every fifth step. Grace went in to her hip once and emerged swearing with a creativity that raised my estimate of her verbal reserves by an order of magnitude. Two parties ahead of us had turned around; a third had snowshoes and floated past us like a taunt.

Decision point at 0947: continue 0.75 mi to the upper lake at an observed pace of ~40 min/mi with worsening snow, or declare the second lake the summit. We took the second option, and here's the thing the spreadsheet can't hold: the second lake was still half frozen, teal water against white ice against gray cliff, with a waterfall coming through a notch above it, and calling that a "failure state" would be data malpractice.

## Observations (unstructured)

Wildlife: one moose. I want to log this properly because it was the highlight and moose don't fit in cells. Cow moose, in the willows at the marshy stretch around the first lake, maybe 60 yards off, at 0710. We stopped, backed up, gave her the entire zip code. Moose injure more people in this park than bears do — I'd read that stat, and standing there watching 800 pounds of authority browse willows, I felt it convert from trivia to policy. Also logged: a ptarmigan still mostly in winter white against brown tundra, an extremely findable disguise failure; countless juncos; one weasel crossing the trail like it owed money.

Conditions for those planning: dry and fast to the first lake (2.9 mi by my watch, sign claims 2.7, the eternal reconciliation problem). Mud and running water across the trail in the marshy section — waterproof shoes earned their keep. Snow patchy from ~10,200, continuous and rotten above the second lake. Poles: essential. Snowshoes before 0800 if you're set on the upper lake. Microspikes: brought, barely used, would bring again because 340 grams of insurance is cheap.

Weather held clear until we were down, but cumulus were building over the divide by 1100 in the standard afternoon-thunderstorm pattern this park runs like a cron job. Respect the schedule.

## Lessons learned

1. Snow depth data ≠ snow quality data. My melt model gets a v2 with aspect and time-of-day.
2. Turnaround decisions made at a named landmark feel like arrivals, not aborts. Choose your bailout points to be beautiful.
3. Grace gets veto power on all future itineraries with the word "bench" in them, per a verbal agreement extracted while she was hip-deep in snow. Terms are binding.

## Verdict

Objectives achieved: 2 of 3 lakes, 1 of 1 moose, 1 of 1 burrito. Incident count: zero. The mountain rejected my schedule and offered a better one. I'm marking this ticket resolved, and reopening it in late July, when the model says — with appropriately widened error bars — that the snow will be gone.
