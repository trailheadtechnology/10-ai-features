I rewrote my "in case something happens" note to my roommate three times before my first ever solo hike, which tells you everything about my mental state on the morning of June 14th, and I'm writing this report for every other woman sitting in a trailhead parking lot right now trying to talk herself into opening the car door.

Open the door. That's the whole report. But my journalism degree says I owe you details, so.

The trail: Laurel Gap Vista Trail, picked after two weeks of research that my roommate called "preparing for a moon landing." Criteria: popular enough that I wouldn't be truly alone, short enough to bail easily, pretty enough to be worth the anxiety spiral. It's listed as 3.9 miles round trip — my watch logged 4.2, trailhead sign said "2 mi" to the vista, so pick your truth — climbing steadily, around 850 feet, through rhododendron tunnels to a rock outcrop with a view across about six blue ridgelines, which is apparently the standard Smokies special and I now understand the fuss.

I started at 8:40 a.m. after a 25-minute parking lot negotiation with myself. Things I was afraid of, ranked: bears, men, twisting an ankle alone, being seen crying by strangers, and bears again. Things that actually happened, ranked: I sweated through my shirt in the first mile because June in Tennessee is a wet blanket, I said "good morning" to eleven strangers and all eleven were normal, I ate a cheese sandwich on a rock above the entire visible world, and I laughed out loud alone on a trail for possibly the first time in my life.

The rhododendrons were in full bloom, whole hallways of pinkish-white, and where the trail crosses a little branch there was mountain laurel dropping petals into the water like confetti after a parade everyone left. I stopped there for a while. Nobody needs to know how long.

Bear report, since it was 40% of my fear budget: I did see one. About half a mile below the vista, maybe 150 feet off-trail downslope, a black bear flipping over a log with the focus of someone looking for their keys, completely uninterested in me. I did everything the NPS website drilled into me — stopped, didn't run, spoke in a calm voice that came out two octaves above normal, backed up the trail and waited with a family of five that came up behind me. The bear ambled off in its own direction within a couple of minutes. My heart rate app later showed a spike labeled "workout intensity: peak." Accurate. But here's what I want other nervous first-timers to hear: the scary thing happened, the actual thing, and it was fine. It was better than fine. It was ten seconds of pure awe wrapped in adrenaline.

Practical notes: trail in good condition, one muddy stretch mid-way with good rock-hopping options, roots slick where shaded. The vista rock has plenty of room to sit away from the edge, which matters to those of us whose imaginations are athletes. Haze rolled in by 11, so go early for the view — the famous "smoke" is gorgeous but it does eat the distant ridges by midday. It was about 68°F when I started, low 80s and thick by noon. I drank all 2 liters I carried and bought a Gatorade after with the urgency of someone leaving a desert.

Total time: three hours and ten minutes, including the bear intermission and the sandwich summit. On the way down I passed a woman about my age, alone, doing the exact hesitant walk I'd done that morning, and she asked "is it worth it?" and I got to be the person who said "completely, and you're almost through the steep part." Full circle in one day.

The note to my roommate is still in my glovebox. I'm keeping it there. Not because I'm scared now — because next time I want to read it and remember who I was in that parking lot, versus who came down the mountain. Different person. Same sweaty shirt.

Solo hike number two is already picked out. It's longer.
