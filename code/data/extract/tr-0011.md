Well, we finally did the Yosemite trip, and I've been meaning to write something up ever since, so here goes, better late than never as my mother used to say, usually about my father.

It was early last month when we went, I want to say a weekday, because I remember Carol saying the crowds would be better on a weekday and Carol is generally right about crowds, she has a system. We'd been planning the trip since Christmas, or technically since Carol's brother went two years ago and wouldn't stop talking about it at Thanksgiving, and there's only so many Thanksgivings a man can sit through hearing about somebody else's vacation before he books his own, that's just human nature.

The drive in, I'll tell you, is a whole thing by itself. You come through the tunnel and there's that famous view, everybody knows the one, and every car pulls over including ours, and Carol cried a little, and I pretended I didn't see so she could have her moment, and she pretended she didn't know I saw, which is basically our whole marriage and it works fine.

We did a hike the next morning, one of the waterfall ones. I couldn't tell you the exact name off the top of my head — Carol has it written in the little book, and the little book is in the car, and the car is at the mechanic, which is a story I'll spare you except to say never let your nephew "take a look at" your brakes. Anyway, the waterfall one, the one everybody does, near the lodge. You've seen it on calendars.

How long was it? You know, people always ask that and I never know what to tell them. It took us the morning. We're not fast and we're not slow, we're just steady, and we stop when there's something worth stopping for, which in that place is roughly every forty feet, so the math gets complicated. My knee, the left one, the one from the softball thing in '09, held up better than expected, which I attribute to the new insoles, and Carol attributes to me finally listening to the doctor, and we've agreed to disagree.

The falls themselves were going pretty good, it being spring and all, lots of water coming down, you got wet standing anywhere near the bottom which the kids around us thought was the greatest thing that ever happened. The path was, I'd say, fine? Some wet spots. A fellow ahead of us slipped a little and did that arm-windmill thing but he caught himself, and we all did the thing where you pretend you didn't see, there's a lot of polite not-seeing in this report now that I read it back.

We had lunch after at a picnic area, egg salad, which travels worse than Carol believes it does but I've been losing that argument since the Clinton administration. There was a couple at the next table from Ohio, or maybe he was from Ohio and she was from Indiana, one of those, and we talked for an hour about nothing, the way you do on vacation, and traded phone numbers we'll never use, which is its own kind of nice when you think about it.

The rest of the trip was mostly driving around and looking at things, which suits us. Carol took four hundred photos of the same cliff in different light and I'm not being critical, some of them are terrific, she's got the eye. We got ice cream twice in one day and told nobody until now.

Anyway. It's a beautiful place, the pictures don't do it, and you should go. Take a jacket, the mornings were colder than the afternoons let on. And write things down while you remember them, that's my real advice, don't be like me starting a trip report a month later with the little book in a car at the mechanic's. Next time I'm keeping notes. Carol says she'll believe it when she sees it.
