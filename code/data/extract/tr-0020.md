Okay so for context, I did not want to go on this hike.

My parents have this thing now where family time is mandatory and outdoors, which started after my mom read some book, and on March 7th the mandatory outdoors was an 8-mile hike to a rock called Charlies Bunion, which, first of all, is named after a foot problem. I looked it up in the car hoping it was at least a cool foot problem story, and it kind of is? Apparently in the 1920s a guy named Charlie Conner was hiking there with the writer Horace Kephart after a huge fire and landslide stripped the cliff bare, and Charlie's feet were wrecked, and Kephart looked at this gnarly knob of exposed rock and said basically "that sticks out like Charlie's bunion" and then PUT IT ON A MAP, which is an incredible way to be remembered forever. Charlie didn't even get a say. Anyway that's the best fact in this report, everything else is just nature.

We started at Newfound Gap at like 8am, which is this parking lot on the state line where you can stand in Tennessee and North Carolina at the same time, and yes we did the thing, everyone does the thing. The hike is on the Appalachian Trail, the actual real AT, which I'll admit is lowkey cool — we passed two actual thru-hikers who'd walked from Georgia and smelled like commitment, and one of them called her trail name ("Detour") back over her shoulder and my dad has not stopped talking about trail names since. His would be "Dad Joke." He suggested it himself. The system works.

It's about four miles out and four back, and the first two miles are just up, like 900-ish feet of up, and in early March there was still crusty old snow and straight-up ice in the shady stretches, so everyone was doing the penguin shuffle in the icy parts, and my mom made us wear these little spike things over our shoes and I want it noted that I mocked the spike things at breakfast and by mile one I was pro-spike. Growth.

Here's the thing I wasn't ready for: it was actually stupidly beautiful?? The trail runs along the ridge crest through this dark spruce forest that looks like Canada got lost, everything furred with frost, and there were these gaps where you could see like nine layers of blue mountains in every direction with fog sitting in the valleys like soup. My phone died from the cold at mile three (37 degrees and windy, my battery said "no") which my mom called "a blessing" and honestly, unrelated to her being right, it was kind of nice to just look at stuff.

The Bunion itself is this bare rock outcrop hanging off the side of the ridge with a drop that made my stomach do the elevator thing. There's a sign that says please don't die on it, in politer words, and there's a little path around the back for people who'd rather not test it. My dad went out on the rock. My mom held his jacket. Marriage. We ate peanut butter sandwiches sitting against the rock out of the wind, and a bird straight-up landed on my brother's knee — my mom's app said Canada jay later, apparently they're famous freeloaders — and he sat SO still, this kid who cannot sit still for one second of family dinner, frozen like a statue for a whole bird visit. Also we saw juncos, which I now know are a bird, and a raven that yelled at us, valid.

The four miles back were somehow both easier and eternal, ice melting to slush by afternoon, mud situation upgrading from "some" to "yes," and my legs the next day felt like they'd been replaced with angry pool noodles.

Final review from a former hater: 8 miles, one foot-problem rock, one knee bird, zero bars of signal, and I'd… go again? Don't tell my mom I wrote that. She reads these sites. Hi mom. The spike things were fine, okay. You were right about the spike things.
