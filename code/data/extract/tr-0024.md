Base weight for this trip: 3.71 kg. I want that number up front because everything else in this report flows from it.

Acadia is not a park that demands ultralight discipline — nothing here is more than a few miles from a road, and my day on the Schoodic Ledges Traverse was, let's be honest, a day hike that I treated like an expedition because that is who I am now and my partner has learned to stop asking. But carrying less is not about necessity. It is about the fact that every gram you carry is a decision, and most people have simply never audited their decisions.

The traverse: roughly 7.4 miles as I routed it, mid-August, linking the ledge trails on the quiet side of the park via a connector that follows an old carriage-road spur. Around 1,100 feet of cumulative gain, which Acadia delivers in its signature style, meaning short, rude, granite staircases rather than anything so merciful as a switchback. I started at 5:40 a.m., partly for the famous first-in-the-nation sunrise, mostly because early starts are free speed and free speed weighs nothing.

Gear notes, because I know why you subscribed: I went frameless (obviously), 19-liter pack, and the entire kit for a Maine day hike in August came to that 3.71 including 700 ml of water capacity — heresy to some, but the map showed two reliable streams and both were flowing, one strongly, one a trickle I had to coax with a leaf. Rain shell: 87 grams, and it earned its place, because a squall came through off the Atlantic around 11 and dropped fifteen minutes of sideways drizzle before dissolving into the kind of rinsed-clean sunlight that makes you forgive Maine everything. My partner carried a 340-gram rain jacket. I have not let this go. I will never let this go.

The ledges themselves are pink granite scraped bare by the glaciers, patched with lichen in gray-green continents, and the walking on them is glorious — grippy when dry, treacherous as wet soap in that drizzle. I watched my footing and lost maybe ten minutes to caution. Cheap at the price. Below the third ledge there's a cobble beach where I stopped for lunch (lunch: 412 calories, 71 grams net of packaging, 5.8 cal/g, a personal best for a no-cook day) and watched harbor seals hauled out on a half-tide rock, eleven of them, doing their banana pose. Later, near the summit knob, a peregrine went by at eye level and below me, which is the only correct way to see a peregrine.

Trail conditions were mostly excellent. The connector spur had two blowdowns, both step-overs, and one boggy hundred-meter stretch where bog boards have rotted out and someone will eventually lose a shoe — someone wearing heavy waterproof boots, that is, which hold water like buckets once breached. My mesh trail runners (247 g/shoe) were soaked and then dry within the hour. This is the way.

Total time: 4 hours 55 minutes including the seal intermission. Water consumed: about 1.6 liters, refiltered en route (filter: 42 grams, and yes I weighed it after the trip too; it gains about 11 grams wet, a fact I share at parties, which may explain the parties I'm invited to).

Would I change anything? I carried a 28-gram emergency bivy I statistically did not need three miles from a road. It stays in the kit anyway. Even zealots have superstitions.

Acadia in August: crowded on the famous side, silent on this one. I saw four humans in five hours, eleven seals, one falcon, and zero regrets. Go light. Go early. Weigh your fears; most of them are heavier than they're worth, and none of them are waterproof.
