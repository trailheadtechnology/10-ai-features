I had never been to a national park before this weekend, so please grade this report on a curve.

My college roommate has been trying to get me to Yosemite for years and Memorial Day weekend was when I finally ran out of excuses. We did the Mist Trail up to Vernal Fall on Saturday the 24th, and I want to write down everything before the soreness fades and I start remembering it as easy.

It is not easy. Everyone online calls it a "short" hike and technically I suppose it is — my roommate said it's about a mile and a half to the top of Vernal Fall and we turned it into a three-mile day by coming back down the same way instead of doing the longer horse-trail loop, which by the end I was very glad about. But nobody warned me that most of that distance is stairs. Hundreds of granite stairs, carved right into the cliff, wet ones, with a five-hundred-some-foot waterfall throwing spray all over you the whole time. In late May the fall was absolutely thundering with snowmelt. "Mist" Trail is false advertising. It should be called the Standing In A Car Wash Trail. I was soaked through my supposedly water-resistant jacket in about ten minutes and my glasses were useless.

And here is the thing: it was the best thing I have ever done.

There's a moment about two-thirds of the way up the staircase where you come around a shoulder of rock and the entire waterfall is just there, right in front of you, and there was a full rainbow sitting in the spray, and I stood there getting drenched and laughing like an idiot. A man coming down said "worth it, right?" and I said "WORTH IT" way too loudly.

Things nobody told me that I will now tell you, fellow first-timers. One, the trail is paved and merely steep up to the footbridge, and that first stretch fooled me into thinking I was fit. Two, everything after the footbridge is the stair section and it is slick — I watched a guy in basketball shoes do a slow-motion split that could have ended much worse. My roommate made me buy actual hiking shoes the week before and I owe her an apology for complaining about the price. Three, the squirrels at the top of the fall are shameless criminals. One unzipped the outer pocket of a woman's backpack while we all watched. Unzipped it. With its hands. We also saw a mule deer on the walk back near Happy Isles, standing in the shade like it was posing for a postcard, and about a thousand steller's jays, which I now know are the loud blue ones.

Four, and this is the big one: start early. We were on the trail by 7am and it felt busy but okay. When we came down around 10:30 the line of people heading up looked like airport security the day before Thanksgiving.

The top of the fall has this smooth granite shelf where everyone flops down to dry out in the sun like seals, and there's a terrifying green pool above the falls called the Emerald Pool with signs everywhere saying do not swim, people have died, and while we ate our sandwiches we watched two separate groups think about it and thankfully chicken out.

My legs went full jello on the descent. Going down wet granite stairs uses muscles I did not know I had and could not name now. Two days later, sitting down onto a chair is a controlled fall. I regret nothing.

Final verdict from a brand-new hiker: I get it now. I finally get it. I've already made my roommate promise to take me back in the fall to try the hike up to Nevada Fall, which is apparently twice as far and twice as steep, and honestly the fact that I'm excited about that instead of horrified probably says everything about how this weekend went.
