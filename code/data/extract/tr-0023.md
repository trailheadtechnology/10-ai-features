# Troop 442 Does Glacier, Day Two: Trail of the Cedars

Eleven scouts, three adults, one trail, zero incidents requiring the first aid kit. By the standards of Troop 442 that is a historic achievement and I want it in the permanent record.

For anyone finding this while planning a trip with kids: we did the Trail of the Cedars on July 12th as the "recovery day" hike between two bigger days, and it was perfect for that. It's short — under a mile, a loop, maybe 0.9 if you believe the sign, and mostly boardwalk — but do not let the mileage fool you into skipping it. This was the hike the boys talked about at the campfire, not the long one.

## The actual report

We got to the trailhead area off Going-to-the-Sun Road about 8:15 in the morning, which I recommend, because when we finished around 10 the parking situation had become a contact sport. The loop is flat, wheelchair and stroller accessible on the boardwalk sections, and it runs through a stand of western red cedars and hemlocks that feels like it got misplaced from the Pacific Northwest. Some of those trees are several hundred years old and close to a hundred feet tall. The air is noticeably cooler and damper in there than anywhere else we went in the park.

Halfway around, the trail crosses Avalanche Creek where it comes shooting out of a narrow gorge, and this is the showstopper. The water is a color I told the boys to write down in their trip journals without using the word "blue." Best answers: "toothpaste," "glacier Gatorade," and from Marcus, our resident poet, "the color of cold." The gorge walls are red rock, sculpted smooth, and the creek absolutely thunders through there in July with the snowmelt still coming down. This is also where the Avalanche Lake Trail splits off for those doing the longer hike up to the lake — we didn't, not with this group, but plenty of people were heading up.

## Scout-leader logistics

- Bathrooms at the trailhead. Use them. You know why I'm telling you this.
- The boardwalk has railings but an eleven-year-old considers a railing a suggestion. We ran a buddy system and put an adult at the back.
- Interpretive signs along the loop. I bribed the scouts with a Snickers for whoever could answer the most questions at the end. Devon won. Devon always wins.
- Total time for us: about 90 minutes for less than a mile, because we stopped constantly, and stopping constantly is the whole point of this trail.

## Wildlife and conditions

No bears, thankfully — though the ranger at the campground reminded us this is very much bear country and we did our whole bear-spray talk anyway, adults carrying. The boys spotted a snowshoe hare in its brown summer coat just off the boardwalk, sitting so still that half the troop walked right past it. Lots of birds we heard and couldn't see; one of the dads has the Merlin app and it claimed varied thrush and golden-crowned kinglet, which I'm taking on faith. Water was seeping across the paved section in two spots near the creek, and the boardwalk gets slick where it's shaded and wet, which is most of it. One scout tested this with the seat of his pants. He's fine. His dignity is not.

Weather was cooperative: low 50s when we started, low 70s and sunny by the time we finished. Mosquitoes present but not biblical.

## Verdict

If your troop, your family, your grandma, or your own tired legs need an easy day in Glacier, this is the one. It packs more per step than any trail I've hiked. Marcus's journal entry ended with "I didn't know trees could be that serious," and honestly, Marcus said it better than I can.
