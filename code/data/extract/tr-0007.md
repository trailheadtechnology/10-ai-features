# Sperry Chalet Trail, July 4, 2026 — full trip log

I keep detailed notes on every hike because six months later I never trust my memory, and this one deserved careful notes. On Saturday, July 4, 2026, I hiked the Sperry Chalet Trail out of the Lake McDonald Lodge trailhead, and it was the hardest and best day I've had in Glacier.

The numbers first, since that's what I always want from other people's reports. It's 6.4 miles one way from the trailhead to the chalet, so 12.8 miles round trip the way I did it, and the elevation gain is about 3,400 feet — the trailhead sits near 3,200 feet by Lake McDonald and the chalet perches at roughly 6,560. I started walking at 6:40am, reached the chalet at 10:55, left the chalet at 12:10 after a long lunch, and was back at the car at 3:25pm. Moving time per my watch was 5 hours 51 minutes. Temperature was 54 degrees at the trailhead and about 66 at the chalet with a steady cool breeze, one of those flawless Glacier summer days.

The trail climbs immediately and does not really stop. The first couple of miles switchback up through dense cedar and hemlock forest, shady and humid, and this stretch was in excellent shape — dry tread, recently cleared, with fresh sawcuts on two big deadfall logs that must have come down in the spring. Around mile three the forest starts to open and you get into the burned area from years back, snags standing silver against the green regrowth, and the views down to Lake McDonald open up behind you. From about mile four onward you're in open subalpine terrain, crossing several streams. Two of the crossings still had substantial snowmelt flow — Sprague Creek in particular was running fast and I was glad for trekking poles, though it never got above mid-calf and there were good rock lines. One lingering snow patch crossed the trail in a shaded gully around mile 5.5, maybe forty feet of it, well-stomped and soft by mid-morning, no traction needed. Otherwise conditions were dry and solid the whole way.

Now the wildlife, which made the day. At 8:20am, in the burn zone, I watched a black bear sow with two cubs feeding on the slope maybe 200 yards above the trail. I stopped, made noise, and she never so much as looked at me; the cubs were wrestling. I gave them ten minutes and moved on with my bear spray unholstered but never close to needed. Higher up, in the cliffs past mile five, mountain goats — I counted nine total, including two kids, one group bedded on a ledge directly above the trail so that I got rained on lightly by pebbles. At the chalet itself a hoary marmot has clearly appointed itself concierge and screamed at every arriving party from the same rock. Mule deer in the trees near the bottom on my way out, four of them. And all day, the whistling of ground squirrels in the rocks.

The chalet is worth the climb even if, like me, you're only day-hiking and not staying over. The stone buildings sit on a knife-edge bench with Glacier spread out below, and they sell lemonade and pie to day hikers, which after 3,400 feet of climbing is not a purchase, it's a religious experience. Huckleberry pie, for the record. I sat on the wall with my boots off for an hour.

Gear notes for my own future reference: poles essential for the creek crossings, sunscreen non-negotiable once you leave the trees, 2.5 liters of water was exactly right with a top-up filter at the mile-four stream. Crowds were light for a holiday — I passed maybe twenty people all day, most of them overnight guests coming down.

If you get one big day in Glacier and your legs are up to it, this is my vote. Nearly thirteen miles, three thousand four hundred feet, three bears, nine goats, one outstanding pie. The notebook doesn't lie.
