# Avalanche Lake, finally, and a full gear debrief

Base weight matters more than people admit, and this trip proved it. I did Avalanche Lake Trail on July 12th with a completely reworked kit and I want to get all of it down while it's fresh, because half the value of a hike like this is the shakedown.

First, the trail itself, for those who skim. You pick it up off Trail of the Cedars, that boardwalk loop through the big old-growth stuff, and from there it's something like two point three miles each way to the lake — call it four and a half, maybe a hair more, round trip. Elevation gain is modest, in the neighborhood of seven hundred feet, spread out gently enough that I never dropped below a three-mile-an-hour pace even with photo stops. Early on you cross the footbridge over Avalanche Creek where it churns through that narrow gorge, and it is worth stopping there even if the crowds bunch up. The water is that milky glacial turquoise that no phone camera gets right.

Now the gear, which is why you're really here.

Pack: I finally retired the old 65-liter monster for a 24-liter frameless day pack, 14 ounces empty. For a half-day walk like this, carrying a framed pack is like driving a moving truck to the grocery store. The frameless rode beautifully. One complaint: the side pockets are cut too tight to reach a bottle without a shoulder dislocation.

Footwear: trail runners, not boots. I know, I know, ankle support, blah blah. The tread was overkill honestly — the trail was in fine shape, packed dirt and roots, a couple of muddy patches maybe a mile in where a seep crosses the tread, nothing a normal human can't step around. Boots would have been four extra pounds of regret.

Water: I carried a liter and a 3-ounce squeeze filter and refilled at the lake inlet. Filtered cold glacial water on a warm July afternoon is one of life's genuinely free luxuries. People hauling three liters from the trailhead for a five-mile day, I salute your training regimen.

Layers: started at 52 degrees at the trailhead around 8am, wind shirt on, wind shirt off within twenty minutes. At the lake there was a breeze coming off the water and the snowfields on the headwall, and the wind shirt earned its 2.1 ounces all over again. There were still white streaks of snow way up on the cliffs above the lake feeding the waterfalls, which in mid-July tells you what kind of snow year it was.

Wildlife, since my wife always asks: one mule deer browsing completely unbothered about fifty feet off the trail near the halfway point, and a whole comedy troupe of ground squirrels at the lakeshore who have clearly been fed by tourists and have lost all fear and most of their dignity. One made a serious run at my hip belt pocket. No bears, though a ranger at the trailhead said there'd been a black bear crossing the road near Lake McDonald earlier in the week. Bear spray stayed on the belt, where it should be — that's one item I will never count grams on.

Crowds: heavy by 10am. If you want the lake to yourself, be walking by 7:30. On my way out around 11 the inbound traffic was a conga line, strollers and flip-flops and one gentleman carrying a full-size bluetooth speaker, may his batteries die swiftly.

Verdict on the kit: total pack weight with water was just under six pounds and I forgot I was wearing it, which is the whole point. Verdict on the trail: an embarrassment of scenery for the effort. Ten waterfalls threading down a two-thousand-foot cirque wall into a lake the color of antifreeze, and all it asks of you is a morning. I'll be back with the tripod and a longer lens, and possibly with the side pockets of that pack re-sewn.
