In Hungary we have mountains but they are polite mountains. The Rocky Mountain is not polite. This I learned on 29 July on the trail called Chasm Meadows Overlook Trail, and I am writing this report because the internet helped me to plan and now I give back, even if my English is going sideways sometimes. Please forgive.

First thing. The altitude is real and it does not care about your opinion. I am coming from Budapest, which is sitting at maybe 100 meters, and the trailhead here is already at 2,900 meters or so — the sign said 9,600 feet, I did the mathematics in my head wrongly two times before my wife Kata corrected. We arrived to Colorado only two days before. This was a mistake. Everybody says acclimate and we said yes yes of course, and then we did not, because we are on holiday and holidays make you stupid.

The trail is about 8 kilometers going and coming back — the Americans said "five miles round trip" and also "5.2" and also "call it five-ish," so who can know — with maybe 450 meters climbing. On paper for us this is nothing. We walk more on a Sunday at Normafa. But on paper there is oxygen.

We started at 7 in the morning because everyone warns about the afternoon thunder storms, and this warning is correct and serious, more about this later. The first part goes through forest of lodgepole pines, very quiet, smelling like a sauna shop. Then the trees become smaller and more heroic, twisted like they are arguing with the wind, and then no trees at all, only tundra with small flowers that a lady on the trail told us survive the winter under the snow. I touched nothing. The signs ask you to stay on trail because the tundra needs one hundred years to forgive one footstep. One hundred years! In Hungary we cannot keep a government one hundred weeks.

The marmots. Nobody prepared me for marmots. They are fat golden cats of the mountain and they whistle at you like construction workers. One was standing on a rock next to the trail, maybe two meters, completely without fear, judging my backpack. Kata has 40 photos of this one marmot. Also we saw pikas, which are small and round and scream "eep" and run with grass in the mouth like they are late for work, and far away on a slope, elk, a whole group, maybe 20.

The overlook itself: I will not try the poetry. There is a cliff wall across the valley, gray and enormous, with snow still in the cracks in the end of July, and below a green meadow with a small lake like a coin. We ate our sandwiches there. A chipmunk performed a robbery attempt on Kata's sandwich. Unsuccessful, but I respected the ambition.

Now the storm warning, because this is the important part of my report. At maybe 11:30, coming down, still above the trees, the sky in the west changed from friendly to lawyer very fast. We heard first thunder at 11:50. We were walking quickly and we were lucky, we reached the forest before the rain, but two young men in shorts were still going UP at noon, to the top, above all trees, and when I said about the storm one showed me his phone application like the phone will stand between him and the lightning. The rangers say be below the tree line by noon. Please, be below the tree line by noon. The mountain does not read your application.

Conditions in short if you skip my rambling: trail dry and in good condition, one snow patch remaining near the top, easy to cross, some mud after where a small stream crosses the path. Toilet at trailhead, ugly but existing. Parking full already at 7:00, we got almost the last place.

Total time for us: five hours with much photographing and much breathing. My legs were fine. My lungs wrote a formal complaint. I would do it again tomorrow.

America, your mountains are rude and magnificent. The marmot can keep my trail mix, he took it fairly when I was not looking.
