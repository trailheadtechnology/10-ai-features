Before I tell you about the trail, I need to tell you about the granola, because the granola is honestly load-bearing for this whole story.

I make a trail granola that my hiking group calls The Contract, because once you've had it you're obligated to hike with me again. The recipe, since three people have asked and I keep promising: four cups old-fashioned oats, one cup chopped pecans, half a cup of pepitas, a heavy half cup of real maple syrup — the dark kind, grade A dark robust, do not bring me amber, amber is for people who iron their jeans — a third of a cup of melted coconut oil, a big pinch of flaky salt, a teaspoon of cinnamon, and here's the move: a quarter teaspoon of cardamom and the zest of one whole orange, stirred in AFTER baking. Bake at 300 for about 40 minutes, stirring once at the 20-minute mark, then — this is the other move — do not touch it while it cools. The not-touching is where the clusters come from. Patience is an ingredient. Add a cup of dried cherries at the very end. Tart cherries. We are not raisin people in this group and we never will be.

Anyway. Glacier, June 24th, and the plan was Avalanche Lake, which had been on our group's list all spring.

The plan did not survive contact with the creek. We got to the trailhead around 8 and there were signs up before we even reached the split from Trail of the Cedars: the footbridge over Avalanche Creek washed out about a week back — a volunteer at the sign said the 18th — and the trail to the lake is closed, full stop, no lake for anybody until the water drops and a crew can rebuild. You could hear why from a quarter mile off. The creek was in an absolute state, this huge white-green roar through the gorge, and we stood at the overlook you can still legally reach and watched it hammer through the rocks, and Denise said "yeah no, that would simply eat us," and Denise summarizes things well.

So we audibled, which is what the granola is for, honestly — morale infrastructure. We did the full Trail of the Cedars loop first, which even as a consolation prize is kind of magnificent, these ancient cedars and hemlocks with the boardwalk winding under them, cool and dim and smelling like a lumberyard in heaven. Under a mile, flat, and we took an hour anyway. Then we drove over and hiked the Johns Lake loop area on the other side of McDonald Creek to make a real morning of it, another couple of easy miles, trail dry and clear, mosquitoes present and enthusiastic, be advised.

Wildlife accounting for the day: one mule deer in the cedars, standing in a shaft of light like it was being paid for the ad. A pileated woodpecker, heard then seen, working a snag near Johns Lake. Harlequin ducks! Two of them, riding the whitewater of McDonald Creek like it was nothing, which is their entire deal and it never stops being absurd to watch — that water was throwing full-sized logs around and these little painted ducks are just bobbing through it, commuting.

We ate lunch on the rocks by McDonald Creek — sandwiches, and The Contract passed around in its big jar, and a woman from another group drifted over with the exact face of someone following a smell, and left with the recipe dictated into her phone, so the granola's range continues to expand. Cardamom, I told her. After baking. She repeated it back like a vow.

Real talk for anyone with Avalanche Lake plans this summer: don't, not yet. The bridge is genuinely gone and the closure is genuinely enforced and the water is genuinely trying to kill people. Check the park's conditions page before you drive out; word at the trailhead was it'll be closed well into July at minimum. The cedars and the McDonald Creek side are open and lovely and nobody who spends a morning there with decent snacks is having a bad day.

Six-ish easy miles across all the wandering, zero lakes, two harlequin ducks, one recipe successfully propagated. The lake keeps. The Contract endures.
